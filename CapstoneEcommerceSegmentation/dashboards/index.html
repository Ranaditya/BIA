Method & interpretation

How transaction lines become customer segments, and what conclusions the analysis supports.

Analysis notes
01

Prepare transaction history

Load dates as datetimes and customer IDs as nullable strings. Remove missing customer IDs, cancellation invoices, prices below £0.01, non-numeric stock codes, and exact duplicates. Calculate line value as quantity multiplied by unit price.

02

Aggregate customer behavior

Build Recency in days from the latest purchase to the snapshot, Frequency from distinct invoice numbers, and Monetary from summed line values. Apply log1p and standard scaling before distance-based clustering.

03

Compare segment structures

Scan KMeans cluster counts using inertia and silhouette, then compare KMeans, Ward hierarchical clustering, and DBSCAN. Consider profile clarity and stability in addition to a single separation score.

04

Validate the right question

The Random Forest currently learns to reproduce KMeans labels from the same RFM inputs. Its holdout score is not future-purchase prediction. A forecast needs an explicit future outcome and a time-based holdout.

RFM definitions

Recency
Elapsed days since the customer's latest invoice. Lower values mean more recent activity.
Frequency
Count of distinct invoices for the customer, not the number of item rows.
Monetary
Sum of transaction-line values, calculated as Quantity × UnitPrice.

Use results responsibly

  • The data covers a historical period; patterns may not describe current customers.
  • One year of monthly observations is not enough to establish recurring seasonality.
  • Heuristic segment names are summaries of profiles, not ground-truth customer types.
  • Test retention campaigns with a control group before attributing business impact.
